# 1794 Dossier: Shadows of Vilnius

**Conventions.** All dates are Gregorian, the calendar the Commonwealth used. Russian sources use the Julian calendar, which was 11 days behind in 1794, so their dates must be converted. Confidence labels:
- **Confirmed**: checked against a reliable source.
- **Secondary**: rests on a popular-history source, an institutional web page or a single secondary source.
- **Unverified**: not confirmed, or the sources conflict (the conflict is stated).
- **Computed**: our own astronomical calculation; check it against an ephemeris.

**The city in 1794.** Vilnius was a late-baroque city, rebuilt by J. C. Glaubitz and others after the fires of 2 Jun 1737, 11 Jun 1748, 8 Jun 1749 and 28 May 1760.
- The 1760 fire burned 557 properties, the largest count of the century (B. Manyś, *Lietuvos istorijos metraštis* 2017/2). It started beyond the Gates of Dawn, so façades at the south end were about 30 years old in 1794. Elsewhere they were 30–55 years old, built on Gothic and Renaissance cellars.
- The spine streets were almost entirely masonry.
- In 1790, 35 of about 300 plots under the magistrate stood empty (Klovas, Meilus, Valionienė, Lithuanian Institute of History; Confirmed). Include some gutted shells and vacant plots.
- Population was about 17,450 (official 1796 count), roughly 35–40% Jewish (Unverified).

## 2. Back-dating rules

**Strip:**
- **Added storeys.** These are dated per building in the heritage register (KVR) and range from 1718 to the late 20th century. Never remove a top storey blindly.
- **Roofs.** Mansards and dormer rows date from the second half of the 19th c. Low 19th-c. roofs (27–37°) should be steepened.
- **Historicist façades** date from the late 19th c. to 1910.
- **Balconies, large-pane windows, glazed shopfronts, signage and utilities.** No Vilnius-specific dates exist (Unverified). Remove them by default and turn ground floors back into vaulted openings with timber shutters.
- **Landmark accretions.** The Cathedral statues and saints; the belfry top (after 1798) and roof (1893); the Gates of Dawn façade (1829), gallery (1830?) and stairs (1879); St Anne's tower (1873); Neo-Byzantine work (1845–65); the 19th-c. garden on Town Hall Square.
- **Post-war work.** Didžioji 10's façade (1999), Didžioji 19 (1973), the widening of Vokiečių.

**Add back:**
- the wall and gates, and the Lower Castle precinct
- St Paraskeva as a ruin
- vacant plots and gutted shells
- market stalls and punishment structures
- the frescoes on St Johns'
- the houses demolished around St Nicholas (1865) and in front of the Chodkiewicz Palace (1825–34)

**Typology rules when evidence is missing** (grade every output):
- **R1.** Take modern eave and ridge heights from LiDAR, and storey counts from KVR photos.
- **R2.** If a storey addition is dated before 1794, keep the storey; if after, remove it (grade A).
- **R3.** Clues to a later top storey: a historicist remodel on a 16th–17th-c. core, a different window rhythm on the top floor, or an old eaves cornice now reading as a string course. In that case use the current storey count minus one (B).
- **R4.** Default spine house: 2 storeys plus cellar and a steep tiled roof. Palaces and large corner houses get 3; courtyard wings 1–2 (C).
- **R5.** Height of a removed storey: the median of A-graded neighbours. Calibrate on the roughly 30 buildings with dated events.
- **R6.** Gable or hip roof in hand-made clay tile at about 45°. The pitch rests on one secondary source (C). Take ridge direction from Kunicki's 1805 plan, Smuglewicz and Wilczyński.
- **R7.** Churches, palaces, the Town Hall and the gates are always modelled from evidence, never from typology.
- **R8.** Do not level the eaves lines.

**Colour.**
- Churches and palaces: the 1743 paint layer found on St Catherine's gives red-ochre walls, warm-white pilasters and frames, and grey reliefs. Dark "Pompeian gold" is also documented (Klajumienė).
- Houses: no study was found. Use limewash in whites, ochres and greys with painted trim (C).
- Do not copy today's colours. They reflect 19th-c. Russian-era repainting and later restorations.

## 3. Street-level life and night

- **Paving.** Rounded fieldstone cobbles and open drainage channels (on Pilies by 1642), clogged with mud and dung. A paving toll was levied in 1791. No raised pavements or kerbs are documented; kerb rows appear in the late 19th c. (Secondary.)
- **Water.** Bored-log pipes carried spring water to public cisterns with wooden lids (*rurmusai*) on Town Hall Square, at the Lower Castle, on Vokiečių and by St Johns' belfry. There were also private wells, and no fountains. (Secondary.)
- **Markets.** Town Hall Square held timber and brick stalls, shops inside the Town Hall, warehouses around it, and a day-labourers' hiring spot. From 1785, trading on Aušros Vartų was banned during services, and everyone had to walk bareheaded from the gate to the Pociej chapel (Confirmed, ausrosvartai.lt). The 1742 and 1783 privileges barred new Jewish residence on the Gates of Dawn–Cathedral route (Secondary). Shop signs and vehicles are Unverified.
- **The watch.**
  - Before 23 April: Russian musketeers (Narva and Pskov regiments), the main guardhouse, gate guards and Cossack patrols.
  - 23 April–12 August: Lithuanian regulars, plus a burgher city guard from 1 May.
  - After 12 August: the Russian garrison, with the old magistrate structures still in place. The Russian police and the abolition of Magdeburg law came in **1795**, so leave them out of autumn 1794.
  - Curfews, passwords and the autumn regiments are Unverified.
- **Lighting (Unverified; sources conflict).** Gotalskis and Klimka (*Mokslo ir technikos raida Lietuvoje* 10, 2006, pp. 304–313) describe 18th-c. hemp-oil street lamps burning 15 Sep–15 Apr under keepers. An archive-based article (neakivaizdinisvilnius.lt) says the main streets got lanterns only in the early 1800s. Later dates are firm: 28 lanterns in 1824 and gas on 22 Oct 1864. The magistrate records in the Lithuanian State Historical Archives (LVIA, fond 458) would settle it.

**Art-direction consequences:**
- There is no rhythm of lamp posts. Light pools come from hand lanterns and torches (patrols, servants), carriage lamps, small-paned windows lit by tallow or wax candles, guard-post fires, and church and shrine lamps. A lamp burning before the Gates of Dawn icon at night is Unverified.
- Optional, and flagged as a choice: a few wall-bracket oil lanterns at the Town Hall, guard posts and palaces.
- Patrols carry their own light, so the player sees them before being seen. Wet cobbles pick up sparse warm points between long dark gaps. Moon phase matters (see §4).
- Time is told by bells. The Town Hall has no clock; whether the Cathedral belfry clock worked in 1794 is Unverified.

**Material vocabulary.**
- **Use:** hand-made red clay tile (with occasional green, yellow or lead-grey glaze); copper or iron sheet only on domes, spires and palaces; lime plaster over brick; fieldstone; timber shutters and stalls; wooden pipe and cistern lids; wrought-iron crosses and finials; scaffolding and brick stacks at the Cathedral and the Town Hall.
- **Exclude:** cast iron, plate-glass shopfronts, lamp posts, asphalt, granite kerbs, zinc roofs, mansards.

**Per-building method:**
1. Start from the GRPK footprint.
2. Join it to KVR.
3. Harvest the KVR record.
4. Read the acts and queue scan orders.
5. Georeference the 1737, 1805, 1808 and 1944 sources.
6. Sweep iconography under historical names.
7. Take metrics from LiDAR.
8. Apply back-dating operations, each tied to evidence IDs.
9. Grade each feature and record paradata.
10. Keep a separate gameplay-deviation layer, then send for historian review.

**Registry record** (one per building):
- **Identifiers:** `building_id`; geometry (`grpk_top_id`, `osm_ids`, `footprint_1794_status`, `plot_ref_1805`); addresses (current and historical, 1790s owner).
- **Heritage:** KVR codes and GUIDs, status, dating text, style, authors.
- **Modern state:** eave and ridge heights, storeys, year built.
- **`state_1794`:** exists, storeys, heights, roof form, pitch and material, façade, window axes, ground-floor use, gateway, colour.
- **History and operations:** `alteration_history[]` and `backdating_ops[]`, each with `evidence_ids`.
- **`evidence[]`:** type, creator, date, holder, call number, URL, licence, reference-only or redistributable, view direction.
- **Confidence per feature** (massing, height, roof, façade, openings, details, colour):
  - A = documented up to about 1800, or a dated post-1794 change.
  - B = inferred from 1805–1870s evidence plus typology.
  - C = typology only.
  - Map these to the Apollonio 7+1 uncertainty scale and London Charter paradata.
- **Gameplay:** climbable faces, rooftop routes, gateways, interior, scale deviation, anachronism flags.
- **Workflow:** status, reviewer.

## 7. Sensitivity guidance

- **Sacred sites.** The Gates of Dawn, the Cathedral, St Casimir's, Holy Spirit, Holy Trinity and the Great Synagogue are all active or protected sites.
  - No combat, looting or damage inside them.
  - The player never harms the icon; show the 1794 damage as aftermath only.
  - Keep the chapel roof and the icon window off-limits to parkour.
  - Consult the Archdiocese of Vilnius and the rector of the shrine.
- **Jewish community.**
  - Give Jewish characters agency: the community council's donation, the tailors, defenders and scholars.
  - Avoid spy, innkeeper and collaborator tropes.
  - Label the Gaon's cannonball story as a legend.
  - The future of the Great Synagogue site is publicly disputed (Jerusalem Post/JTA, 5 Aug 2026). Consult the Lithuanian Jewish Community and the Vilna Gaon Museum.
- **Kossakowski's execution.** Show it as a public event that people witnessed and argued over. Do not make the player the executioner, and add no mockery or invented inscriptions.
- **National readings.**
  - Say "Lithuanian army" or "Commonwealth forces", never "the Polish army".
  - Give names in both forms (Jasiński/Jasinskis; Wilno/Vilnius/Vilne/Vilna).
  - Present the city as many-faith and many-language.
  - Players will read Russian enemies through the lens of the war in Ukraine, so give them faces, using Tuchkov as a model.
- **Legends and dates.** Label every legend (Deev's Carmelite, the icon damage). Normalise all Julian dates.
